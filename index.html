1 · Understanding the issue

Will AI take over the call center?

What the issue is, and why it matters

My issue sits under the globalization driver of technology: automation and AI replacing jobs in the Philippine BPO industry, and the common belief that AI will fully take over those jobs. It matters because about 1.9 million Filipinos work in the industry, which brings in roughly US$40 billion a year (BPO and Beyond, 2026). Many are young, many work nights, and many are first-time earners supporting a household.

The industry body IBPAP lowered its 2028 worker target from 2.5 million to between 1.85 and 2.14 million (Macayan, 2026a), and reported job growth has slowed to about 3% (PSA Intelligence, 2026). The risk is real. The question is how large it is and who gets hit first.

Causes, effects and stakeholders

Tap or hover a card to open it.

+Cause

AI tools take routine tasks

Generative AI now handles work agents used to do by hand.

More than two-thirds of IBPAP member companies already run AI pilots (People Matters, 2026). Training is shifting from communication skills toward AI tools and data skills.

+Cause

Cost pressure

Cutting staff is the quick way to save money.

Redeployment is voluntary, so cost pressure may push companies to cut anyway. Some layoffs may also be blamed on AI when weak demand is the real cause (People Matters, 2026).

+Effect

Entry-level roles feel it first

Slower hiring and a smaller 2028 target.

Job growth is down to about 3% (PSA Intelligence, 2026). The workers most exposed are those in routine, entry-level seats, which is also where most young workers start.

+Effect

Uneven access to training

Not every worker gets upskilled.

One of my interviewees, Aeon, said training at his company is still "more of a wish." Reports also warn that reskilling may not keep pace with automation (Khan, 2026).

+Stakeholders

Who has a stake

IBPAP, DICT, DOLE, TESDA, BPO companies, workers, and Singapore as a comparison case.

IBPAP is the industry body and has committed at least US$25 million a year to workforce development (Nearshore Americas, n.d.). DICT leads the government's AI-readiness push and aims to upskill more than 300,000 BPO workers (People Matters, 2026). DOLE handles labor policy; Undersecretary Carmela Torres said "AI is transforming jobs rather than outright replacing them" (Macayan, 2026b). TESDA runs the AI upskilling program through employer-based training, with DICT, under Project Unlad (Macayan, 2026b). SkillsFuture Singapore is my comparison case for supporting displaced workers (Free Malaysia Today, 2025).

What the interviews added

I interviewed five BPO workers. Their accounts matched the reports on one point: the technology is arriving unevenly.

Training at his company is still "more of a wish."Aeon, BPO worker (interview)

Another interviewee, Joy, described her employer removing an AI tool and having staff handle the task again. Both show that AI use in BPO is still being tested, which supports my position that preparing workers matters more than predicting mass job loss.

Sources I used

I used industry and government reporting (Outsource Accelerator, People Matters, PSA Intelligence, BPO and Beyond, Nearshore Americas), a Singapore policy report (Free Malaysia Today), and my own interviews. Where two reports disagree, I say so instead of picking one. Reports put the number of workers to be upskilled at over 300,000 and at up to 340,000 a year. The full list is in References.

2 · My proposed approach

Industry-led reskilling, backed by redeployment

IBPAP and its member companies train current BPO workers in AI tools and human skills such as empathy and complex problem-solving. Companies move staff whose tasks AI takes over into new roles instead of cutting them.

Why it works

It answers the real risk directly. IBPAP is already funding training, the government-backed track targets 10,000 BPO workers trained in AI in 2026 and 34,000 more through partner institutions (Macayan, 2026b), and the national goal is about 2 million AI-ready workers (PSA Intelligence, 2026). Redeployment protects the entry-level workers who are exposed first. It also fits DOLE's view that jobs are being transformed, not erased.

Alternatives I considered

Scores 3, 2, 4, 3, 3. Strongest on feasibility because funding and a partner already exist. Weakest on measurable impact, since no results are published yet.
2 · Company policy: redeploy, don't cut2.6 · Needs refinement
Scores 2, 3, 3, 3, 2. Keeps skilled workers, but it is voluntary and there is no industry-wide rule or result yet. I fold it into my main solution.
3 · Income support and retraining (Singapore model)2.4 · Needs refinement
Scores 3, 2, 2, 3, 2. Singapore pays a second-diploma subsidy plus up to S$3,000 a month for two years to workers aged 40 and above (Free Malaysia Today, 2025). It is costly, has no published re-employment figures, and would need new funding here.

How it works in practice

I would start with a pilot at one or two IBPAP member companies and expand only after the results are in.

  1. MONTHS 0 TO 3

    List at-risk roles, update course content, open sign-up to current agents, and name a coordinator at each pilot company.

  2. MONTHS 3 TO 6

    Run hands-on workshops scheduled around night shifts. Track workers enrolled, trained and certified.

  3. MONTHS 6 TO 12

    Match staff whose tasks AI takes over to new roles, with transition training. Review results every quarter.

  4. AFTER ONE YEAR

    Publish the results, refine the approach, then expand to more member companies.

Delivery is split by role. IBPAP and BPO companies run the training with TESDA support. Companies handle redeployment. IBPAP reports results with DOLE oversight. It can draw on Project Unlad, IBPAP's US$25 million yearly commitment, TESDA's skills-training mandate (RA 7796), and CREATE Act incentives (RA 11534).

3 · Evaluating the idea

How I would know it worked

These are proposed targets for the one-year pilot. They are my own planning numbers, not published results.

  • S
    SpecificTrain current entry-level agents at one or two IBPAP member companies in AI tools and human skills, and redeploy those whose tasks AI takes over.
  • M
    MeasurableAt least 80% of enrolled agents complete training. At least 70% of affected workers are moved into new roles instead of cut.
  • A
    AchievableStart small, use existing training budgets, and tap IBPAP's funding and TESDA's employer-based training.
  • R
    RelevantIt supports the national goal of about 2 million AI-ready workers and protects the entry-level roles that are exposed first.
  • T
    Time-boundPilot runs 6 to 12 months. Early indicators at 3 to 6 months, retention and role movement at 12 months, with a quarterly review.

Outcomes I expect

Workers keep more job security, companies keep trained staff and lower turnover, and the industry stays competitive. Retention and promotion rates of trained workers should be measurable about one year after training starts.

Long-term impacts to watch

+Watch

Training that misses real jobs

Skills must match actual openings.

Use IBPAP's skills map and review it every six months. Project Unlad targets AI data annotators, analytics specialists and AI engineers (Macayan, 2026b).

+Watch

Layoffs blamed on AI

Weak demand can look like automation.

Record the reason for each separation so the results are not skewed (People Matters, 2026).

+Watch

Night-shift participation

Workers may be too tired to attend.

Offer flexible and online sessions scheduled around shifts, so the people who most need the training can join.

+Watch

Funding and a backup plan

Start small and keep a fallback.

If AI replaces roles faster than expected, add government retraining and income support from the Singapore model as a second layer.

4 · Presenting my work

Why a website

Format. A website lets me show the issue, the evidence and the numbers in one place, with charts and short cards that readers open when they want detail. It works on a phone, which is where most of my audience will read it.

Audience. A general audience: BPO workers and their families, students considering a BPO job, and the employers and officials who decide on training. No technical background is needed.

Clarity and sources. Each section answers one question in plain language. Every claim carries an in-text citation, interviews are labelled as interviews, and my own planning targets are marked as such. Conflicting figures are shown as conflicting.

Still to verify before final submission: agency websites, RA 7796 and RA 11534, and the worker figures that differ between reports (over 300,000 versus up to 340,000 a year).
